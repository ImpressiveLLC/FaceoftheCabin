# Maintenance and recovery supplement

Audience: operator. Source-reviewed at the corpus baseline; runtime state and recovery results unverified. Read the existing [Maintenance & Operations Guide](../../MAINTENANCE.md) for detailed incident history. This supplement corrects specific source drift and provides a consistent diagnostic sequence.

## First determine what is failing

| Observation | Safe next question/check | What it does not prove |
|---|---|---|
| Page cannot load | Is the intended instance URL reachable, and is its serving/tunnel service healthy? | Backend/database failure is not established merely by a page load failure. |
| Page loads but a feature is empty | Inspect the signed-in browser request/status and the relevant response freshness. | An empty display does not mean no events/devices exist; authorization or network errors may be hidden. |
| Container is running | Inspect its defined healthcheck and the actual feature path. | “Running” is not data flow, authentication, correct configuration or durability. |
| Health reports HA token missing | Check secret presence through the approved provisioning mechanism; do not print it. | A nonblank token is not proof of valid HA authentication. |
| Ask is unavailable or gives raw facts | Check `answeredByModel` and relevant sources, then model availability through an authorized read path. | Fallback is not evidence that the LLM is working. |
| Device is known but offline | Check timestamps, lifecycle, protocol/provider and expected reporting behavior. | Registry existence alone is not a functioning physical device. |
| Presence reads Away (or "Presence unknown") where someone is | Check how old the signal's source is (the badge's age, or the retained `{location}/presence/{person}/last_seen` time), then the phone: is its Tailscale connected (`tailscale status` on the hub lists the phone's `Online` and `LastSeen`), and is the Home Assistant Companion app reporting? | A stale signal is unknown, not away. Do not "fix" it by setting the manual badge: a manual value hides the stale signal. A phone silent for days is the usual cause; one such case (2026-10-04) was a phone off Tailscale since 2026-10-02. |
| The phone WiFi check never reads on | Run `check_authorized_user_phone_wifi.sh <person_id>` inside the Home Assistant container and read its result; a configuration error prints nothing and exits 2 (the sensor then shows unavailable, by design: an error must not read as away). Then compare the MAC in `authorized_user_phones.conf` with the MAC the router shows for the phone on that network. | OFF with the phone on the network usually means the phone presents a different (private) MAC there; that is unconfirmed for the cabin and is an open item (W-37). Do not ask users to change phone settings as the answer. |

Sources: [maintenance incident lessons](../../MAINTENANCE.md), [replication health layers](../../REPLICATION.md#10-monitoring--cross-container-health), [HaTokenCabinHealthIndicator](../../../cabin-orchestration-platform/backend/src/main/java/com/cabin/orchestrator/health/HaTokenCabinHealthIndicator.java), [TinyHelpdeskService](../../../cabin-orchestration-platform/backend/src/main/java/com/cabin/orchestrator/helpdesk/TinyHelpdeskService.java).

Use only established authorized read endpoints for diagnosis. Method names alone are insufficient: [platform import proposals](../../../cabin-orchestration-platform/backend/src/main/java/com/cabin/orchestrator/api/PlatformImportController.java) is a GET that contacts the platform and upserts records. Do not trigger regeneration, scanning, liveview sessions or device commands as an unannounced “read check.”

## A camera is not delivering frames

Audience: operator. Source-reviewed against the live host on 2026-10-03; the Blink row and the detection wording were rechecked against `main` and the Maintenance guide on 2026-10-31 (`NOT_RUN` on the host). The full procedure and the site facts are in the [Maintenance & Operations Guide](../../MAINTENANCE.md#a-camera-on-a-different-network-than-the-m920q).

In plain words, a camera with no video or no events usually has one of four causes: it is a Blink camera, which only streams after Blink itself sees motion, so it is idle most of the time; the Blink bridge has hung; the camera's network address changed; or detection is switched off for that camera, so it records but never makes events. Check them in the order below.

| Observation | Safe next check | What it does not prove |
|---|---|---|
| A Blink camera (the driveway, or the Home Blink camera when it is enabled) shows offline or no frames, and Frigate logs `404 Not Found` for its stream | Look at the Blink bridge's own log: a healthy one logs a motion poll every 30 to 40 seconds (`docker logs -t --tail 1 blinkbridge`). If its last line is minutes or hours old while the container still says "Up", it has hung; `docker restart blinkbridge` has fixed it each time so far. | The 404 alone proves nothing: a Blink camera has a stream only while a live view is open, so an idle, healthy Blink camera logs it every ten seconds. "Up" does not mean the bridge is working. |
| Frigate reports `camera_fps` 0.0 for one camera and its log says "No route to host" | Confirm the camera's current address in its own app or the router's client list, then from the M920q run `ip route get <ip>`, `ping`, and `nc -zv <ip> 554`. | "No route to host" shows that nothing answers at that address. It does not show the camera is off: it may have a new address or sit on another subnet. |
| The camera answers but Frigate still shows no frames | Test the stream from inside the Frigate container with the full ffprobe path `/usr/lib/ffmpeg/7.0/bin/ffprobe`, letting the password expand there. Try a wrong password once, not in a loop. | A reachable camera is not a working stream: paths differ by firmware, and the password may differ from the one in the vault. |
| Frames and recording are fine but the camera never produces events | Read Frigate's effective config for the camera (`/api/config`): is `detect.enabled` true, and which classes does `objects.track` list? | Frames and continuous recording do not mean detection is on. On this cabin's Frigate 0.17, a camera written without a detection setting showed detection off and tracked people only (observed 2026-09-30, W-39; [Maintenance guide](../../MAINTENANCE.md)); set both explicitly. |
| The camera is on a different network from the M920q | Prefer a cable from the M920q's spare Ethernet port to the camera's network. A Wi-Fi join takes the M920q off its own network, Tailscale and the internet. | A network name does not identify a network: names repeat across the cabin, Home and several bands. |

Camera configuration changes go through a pull request, and the camera password never goes into the file or the repository.

## Service health at a glance

A digest of the sections "First determine what is failing", "Running versus healthy, per service" and "What detects and what recovers". Each line points to the section holding the full detail.

- **Four meanings of healthy, in order.** (1) Container running: the main process has not exited, nothing more. (2) Named healthcheck: only a service that defines one can show `(healthy)`. (3) Inter-service path: Uptime Kuma monitors or a cross-container smoke test. (4) The feature itself: a signed-in request or a recent reading. Passing one level does not prove the next.
- **Healthchecks.** Seven Compose healthchecks (`cabin-backend`, `cabin-discovery`, `ollama`, `postgres`, `kafka`, `mosquitto`, `zigbee2mqtt`) plus the Blink bridge's own image check. Thirteen other active services define none and can never show healthy. `depends_on` orders start only; it does not wait for health.
- **Recovery.** Docker `restart: unless-stopped` restarts an exited container, not an unhealthy one. The Blink bridge recovers inside its own loop, and by a host timer only if that is installed. No autoheal container exists, so otherwise an unhealthy container stays unhealthy until a person or a Kuma page acts.
- **First triage.** Page will not load: check the URL and the serving or tunnel service; that does not show the backend is down. Page loads but a feature is empty: inspect the signed-in request status and freshness. Container is running: check its healthcheck and the feature path. Ask gives raw facts: check `answeredByModel` first. Device offline: check timestamps, lifecycle and expected reporting. Presence reads away or unknown: check the age of its source; stale means unknown, not away.

## What detects and what recovers

Detection and recovery are separate. Several layers exist; only some of them restart anything.

| Layer | Detects | Recovers by itself? | Where recorded |
|---|---|---|---|
| Docker `restart: unless-stopped` (9 production-stack services, 9 overlay services) | A container that exited | Yes, restarts it. It does **not** act on an `unhealthy` container, and it does not catch a process that is alive but idle | The two Compose files |
| Docker healthchecks (table above) | One container's minimum useful operation | No. They set the status other layers read | [Replication guide, section 10](../../REPLICATION.md#10-monitoring--cross-container-health) |
| Uptime Kuma monitors | Cross-service paths: MQTT broker, Zigbee2MQTT, backend health, discovery, the camera availability topic, camera frames | No. It pages through the instance's notification route. The reusable monitor set is documented; creating it in production is still approval-pending, so confirm a monitor exists before relying on it | [Replication guide, Uptime Kuma monitor set](../../REPLICATION.md#uptime-kuma-monitor-set) |
| `DeviceHealthMonitor` in `cabin-backend` | Per device, every 60 seconds: stale and missed check-ins; a Home Assistant device is actively polled before it is called missed. Event-driven sensors are exempt from time-based staleness | Marks the device OFFLINE and keeps a per-device reconnect attempt count. It does not restart a container. The result is read at `GET /api/system/health` | `DeviceHealthMonitor.java` |
| Blink motion dead-man alert | The Blink relay going quiet | No. It raises an active alert | `ActiveAlertService`, `BlinkRelayPulse`, #254 |
| Blink bridge rewrite | A failed liveview, throttling, expired token, or a dead loop | Yes, inside the process: per-camera backoff and retry, throttle pause, fresh login, and a watchdog thread that exits the process after 10 minutes of a dead loop so Docker restarts it | Maintenance guide, "blinkbridge rewrite" |
| `blinkbridge-watchdog` host timer | Docker reporting the bridge `unhealthy` (checked every 5 minutes) | Yes, restarts the container. The Maintenance guide called it "staged, not installed"; whether the timer is enabled on the M920q is not established here (`systemctl list-timers blinkbridge-watchdog.timer`) | Maintenance guide, `scripts/host/blinkbridge-watchdog` |
| `infra/watchdog/watchdog.py` | TCP port checks of seven services, publishing `cabin/system/health` | Restarts dead containers, but the M920q overlay sets it `profiles: [disabled]`, so it is not running there | `docker-compose.m920q.yml` |

There is no `autoheal` container. A general "unhealthy then restart" loop exists only for the Blink bridge, and only if its host timer is installed. For every other service, an unhealthy container stays unhealthy until a person or a Kuma page acts.

Three limits to keep in mind:

- `depends_on` in the overlay lists `postgres` and `kafka` without a `condition`, so it orders startup only. It does not wait for either to be healthy.
- A passing Compose healthcheck is not an Uptime Kuma monitor. The Kuma monitor list is the separate record of what is watched (see the Kuma record under persistence above).
- "Every service says running" therefore leaves most of the stack unverified. Pick the services on the path of the feature in question and run its feature check, not all of them.

## Deployment source correction

The old Maintenance Deployment section describes backend deployment as manual-only. The current [deploy-cabin-backend workflow](../../../.github/workflows/deploy-cabin-backend.yml) instead has a main-push path filter and manual dispatch. Its implementation tests, builds/tags a candidate, checks health and has rollback handling. The workflow is the source for current configured behavior; none of this proves its latest run succeeded.

For a release, record separately: repository/commit, tests, built image, target host/stack, deployed image/commit, health result and real feature verification. Obtain authorization for the actual deployment; do not merge, dispatch, rebuild or restart merely because a PR exists. The [family-hub](../../../.github/workflows/deploy-family-hub.yml), [discovery](../../../.github/workflows/deploy-cabin-discovery.yml) and [production stack](../../../.github/workflows/deploy-production-stack.yml) have separate scopes/triggers. Read the relevant workflow rather than applying one generic deployment recipe.

Deploys run on the self-hosted `cabin-m920q` runner; pull-request checks run on GitHub-hosted runners with no host access.

A failed gate stops the run before the rebuild. After the rebuild there is no health check or rollback |
| `deploy-cabin-discovery` | Push touching `discovery-service/**`, the overlay Compose file (any change, unguarded) or itself; manual dispatch | `pytest` in a test image | Rebuilds and restarts `cabin-discovery` | None beyond the container's own healthcheck; no automatic rollback |

Where a document and a workflow disagree, the workflow is the source:

- Older text says the backend deploys by hand only, or that no workflow runs on a pull request. Both are out of date: the backend deploys from a push to `main`, and the tooling and kiosk workflows above run on pull requests.
- A backend-only change under `cabin-orchestration-platform/` also starts `deploy-family-hub`, which rebuilds the UI. A UI-only change does not start the backend deploy.
- Only the backend and the production stack have a post-deploy health check and rollback. The Family Hub, cabin-ui and discovery deploys do not; if one is wrong, redeploy the previous commit.
- Rollback to an earlier backend image exists only while an earlier SHA-tagged image is still on the host. The image prune after a deploy removes untagged images only.

## Persistence and replacement-host recovery

The [base Compose](../../../cabin-orchestration-platform/infra/docker-compose.yml), [M920q overlay](../../../cabin-orchestration-platform/infra/docker-compose.m920q.yml) and [production-stack Compose](../../../cabin-orchestration-platform/infra/production-stack/docker-compose.yml) define different persistence mechanisms. Inspect the selected stack's effective mounts without exposing credentials; service declaration names are not a backup inventory.

| State category | Source starting point | Recovery evidence required |
|---|---|---|
| Application database | Base Compose Postgres volume and backend repositories | Consistent backup, version compatibility and restored application records/behavior |
| Kafka/event processing | Base Compose broker persistence and backend event consumers | Deliberate replay/retention decision; no accidental repeat of external/physical actions |
| Grafana/Prometheus and dashboards | Compose storage and checked-in provisioning | Restored required configuration/history, compatible provisioning and functional dashboards |
| HA, Node-RED, MQTT, Zigbee2MQTT | Selected support stack bind mounts/volumes and device prerequisites | Service configuration, required credential recovery, physical coordinator mapping and verified data path |
| Camera/media services | Production stack media/config mounts and bridge build source | Required media/configuration preserved according to retention policy; streams actually work |
| Ollama/Open WebUI | M920q named volumes | Model/digest and intended chat/config state recover; do not treat model files as application facts |
| Vaultwarden and deployment credentials | M920q vault volume plus the secure provisioning runbooks | Operator-controlled secure backup/decryption recovery; never include contents in this corpus |
| Archives and instance-specific files | Backend archive bind mount and selected stack host mounts | Files, permissions, paths and retention match the new target plan |

This is a **recovery scope**, not a verified backup procedure. Do not copy a live database volume or stop production based on this table. Service-specific consistent backup/restore commands, encrypted off-host custody, recovery objectives and an isolated restore rehearsal remain open in [G04](../corpus/coverage.md#gap-register). A recreated stack with empty/lost required state fails recovery acceptance even if every container is green.

Existing component guidance is available now: [REPLICATION section 10](../../REPLICATION.md#kuma-config-as-code-decision--poc-passed-production-approval-pending) records the Kuma 2.5.0 disposable reconciliation POC and distinguishes a monitor specification from a complete `/app/data` backup. It leaves production reconciliation separately pending and provides a manual monitor target list. Use that current record instead of the older Claude plan's proposed JSON-import wording. For camera/MQTT diagnosis, the same section explains the configured availability topic, frame checks and notification routing; a green service on the wrong broker/topic does not prove the intended path. See [recovered history H06](../corpus/local-history-evidence.md#findings-reconciled-with-current-source).

## A Zigbee plug or sensor is listed twice, charts twice, or shows power as unknown (W-88, 2026-10-21)

Audience: operator. **Why is my plug or sensor listed twice?** One Zigbee device reaches the app twice: once from Zigbee2MQTT and once as one or more Home Assistant entities of the same hardware. The app treats the **Zigbee device as the parent** and each Home Assistant entity as one of its **services**: one card, with the services listed underneath ("Belongs to" in a service's details) and still usable. Devices > **Parent devices only** hides the services. A reading the Zigbee device already reports is logged once, under the Zigbee device, so one sensor makes one chart line; switches and on/off sensors still log under their own entity so workflows watching them keep firing. The app links an entity to its Zigbee device by the device's hardware address when Home Assistant provides it, and otherwise by the entity id starting with the Zigbee device's name at the same location. A name or address two devices share is deliberately not linked, and a parent a person chose is not overridden. Rechecked against `main` on 2026-10-31: `HomeAssistantDiscoveryService` (address first, name prefix as fallback; the duplicate-row skip applies to `sensor` entities only); the ids involved look like `z2m-*` (Zigbee) and `ha-cabin-*` (Home Assistant).

| Observation | Safe next question/check | What it does not prove |
|---|---|---|
| Power, voltage, current or energy read unknown | Is the model supported by the running Zigbee2MQTT? In its device list, `supported: false` with "Automatically generated definition" means it has no converters, so every reading stays `null` and Reconfigure cannot help; a newer Zigbee2MQTT is the fix (found on a new SONOFF S61SZBTPB, 2026-10-21). If it is supported, Z2M sends `null` until the first report: Reconfigure in the Zigbee2MQTT UI and cycle the load once. | An empty reading is not a faulty plug or a platform outage. A Zigbee2MQTT upgrade affects every Zigbee device; back up its data folder first. |
| The Monitoring card has no power tile | Is the device enabled (not just assigned)? Is its type Power meter? Devices registered before 2026-10-21 may carry the old generic type; person-approved re-detect routes exist (W-93: `POST /api/devices/redetect/preview`, `/{deviceId}/redetect/apply`, `/{deviceId}/redetect/rollback`), with no button for them in the app; whether the live device has been corrected is not established here. For a missing On/Off button see [Turn a device on or off from the app](devices.md#turn-a-device-on-or-off-from-the-app). | "Assigned" does not mean enabled or monitored. |
| A chart series stopped on 2026-10-20 or the sensor has two cards, one blank | The Zigbee device is the parent and the Home Assistant entity (often the one a person renamed) is its service. The chart and the card follow the parent; rename the Zigbee device to set the name. The old rows under the entity are still included in the parent's history, and this holds for any parent, including a Home Assistant (`ha-*`) device with linked `sensor.*` entities (#275), not only native Zigbee ones. After a backend restart a battery Zigbee card can stay blank until the sensor next reports (these sensors often report hourly; the interval depends on the sensor and was not verified here). | A blank card is not a dead sensor; wait for its next report, and check **Last seen** and the check-in reason in See, before concluding it is offline. |
| Optimization Opportunities shows "Duplicate readings" | A device and one of its services are both enabled and report the same measurement (found by comparing what each reports, so it applies to any measurement). Monitoring shows the reading once on the parent's tile and the parent's chart includes the service's history. Disable the service unless a workflow needs that entity. | Disabling a service never deletes its history; a resolved opportunity stays resolved until a different service starts repeating the parent. |
| A chart shows two aligned lines for one sensor | Which two device ids? One `z2m-*` and one `ha-cabin-*` of the same hardware are the old double logging; new readings of that measurement go to the parent only. | Old rows remain; they are history, not a current fault. Deleting telemetry is a dry-run script run by the owner, never an agent session. |
