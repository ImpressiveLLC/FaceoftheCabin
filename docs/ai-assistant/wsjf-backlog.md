## Intended product and scope correction

Build an intensely knowledgeable, source-grounded cabin/platform assistant: one in-app place to understand the full supported capabilities, processes, configuration, governance, ontology and technical operation; diagnose problems; receive actionable help; and work through recreating an independently owned instance with the minimum necessary user inputs. Knowledge of the current instance and reusable platform instructions must remain distinguishable. The goal covers Family Hub, cabin orchestration and their supporting services, not only device questions or a vector-search endpoint.

Nate explicitly removed D13–D16 as a contingency. Use verified repository implementation, existing maintained documentation and available approved policy now. A missing handover or decision revision is a source-coverage note; it cannot block unrelated corpus, eval or assistant work. If a specific question truly needs unavailable evidence, mark that answer unknown, link the gap and continue other work. Do not invent a global ratification prerequisite from a missing document.

This correction also supersedes the original pipeline-before-guide sequence: **C2 corpus completion → C1 evaluated LLM/context/retrieval → C3 integrated assistance**. Preserve item identifiers for traceability instead of renumbering old proposals. C4's bounded onboarding endpoint remains independent; it does not by itself provision a complete standalone platform. Shared in-app governance follows the working assistant, with the contribution/evidence contract designed now so knowledge and enhancements are reusable by every authorized collaborator.

“Knowledgeable” means demonstrated coverage and useful help, not a claim of omniscience. When evidence is missing or stale, the assistant explains the limit, offers the next supported step and records a reviewable knowledge gap. It must distinguish explaining an operation, preparing a plan, executing an authorized action and verifying its outcome.

## Shared governance and continuous knowledge

Target: an in-app, role-aware contribution and change workflow open to authorized operators/installers/maintainers, Claude decision/code agents and Codex specialists/collaborators. Reuse the app's identity, policy and review mechanisms where they fit. A contribution's usefulness and evidence determine its treatment, not which model wrote it. Human authority, relevant domain review and safety constraints still apply.

1. **Raise:** from an answer, setup step or capability, any authorized actor can flag an error, supply evidence or propose an enhancement. Preserve context, sources and desired outcome in a shared record with a stable ID.
2. **Develop:** collaborators add competing evidence, proposed docs/code/config/eval changes, affected capabilities, dependency/compatibility impact and validation. Preserve provenance and dissent; never silently replace another actor's work or self-approve a model proposal as policy.
3. **Review:** show role-appropriate reviewers, decision authority and reason for each gate. Ordinary documentation improvements must not wait for unrelated ontology work. Security, safety and ontology changes get the relevant accountable review; pending/running/accepted/rejected/superseded are distinct states with history.
4. **Publish and verify:** GitHub carries the reviewed artifacts and commit/PR evidence. The app references their canonical state instead of inventing a second competing approval ledger. Release changes with their docs and eval updates; verify the actual behavior before marking completed.
5. **Refresh and reuse:** refresh affected knowledge only from the approved version; retain source/index versions, report freshness and regressions, and support rollback. Tell collaborators which reusable capabilities or procedures changed. Failed/stale indexing is visible and cannot silently represent an old corpus as current.

The shared corpus, eval definitions, contracts, decision history and reusable contributions must be accessible to all authorized collaborators in portable, documented forms. Model-specific adapters may differ, but no private agent notebook or vendor-specific project becomes the only source of a platform fact. “Shared” means permission-aware collaboration, not public disclosure of household data or credentials. Plans/proposals can be discussed as such; unapproved proposals never become operational instructions.

This in-app governance enhancement is a follow-on product deliverable, not a capability already provided by the current Ask endpoint or C4. Before its implementation, decompose and score its UI/API/storage/synchronization work in the same shared backlog; do not conceal it within a small documentation or RAG estimate.

## Scoring method and proposed order

```text
CoD = (Safety Value × 2) + Family Usability + Platform Extensibility + Time Sensitivity
WSJF = CoD / Job Size
Each value dimension: integer 1–10
Job Size: relative Fibonacci 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13 (not elapsed days)
```

Scores are planning judgments for the full named item, not measured benefits or ratified priorities. Safety measures reduction in unsafe/misleading guidance and credential exposure, not permission to automate physical actions. Time scores reflect current documented gaps; no deadline or incident is invented. A value score around 3 means limited immediate urgency, 6–7 meaningful recurring value, and 8–10 broad or safety-relevant value. Relative sizes include evaluation, integration, documentation and live verification. C2 and C3 are re-estimated for Nate's clarified scope; their Ratification fields remain unchanged. C2 grows from eleven guide sections to the complete operating/recreation corpus, and C3 grows beyond endpoint plumbing to an in-app assistance journey. Re-score as inventory and baseline evidence improve; unrelated missing decisions are not gates.
